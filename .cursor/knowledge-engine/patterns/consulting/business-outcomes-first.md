## Summary

Buyers purchase revenue, growth, efficiency, automation, cost reduction, and competitive advantage. Technology is the implementation layer — never the headline.

## Core Concepts

| Buyer buys | Not |
|------------|-----|
| Revenue growth | "AI platform" |
| Cost reduction | "Next.js migration" |
| Productivity | "LLM integration" |
| Competitive advantage | "Custom software" |

## Best Practices

- Hero headline states the business outcome in buyer language.
- Feature sections follow: outcome → mechanism → proof.
- CTAs promise a business result ("Get your modernization blueprint") not a deliverable ("Download PDF").
- Case studies lead with metrics, not tech stack.

## Common Mistakes

- Feature-first hero: "We build AI agents with LangChain."
- Jargon without translation: "RAG pipeline" without "faster decisions."
- Listing services without connecting to buyer pain.

## Decision Framework

**When to use:** Every customer-facing page, proposal, audit executive summary, sales deck.

**When not to use:** Deep technical architecture docs for engineering teams (still include business context in intro).

## Examples

- Weak: "Enterprise AI agent development with MCP integration."
- Strong: "Cut approval cycles from days to hours with autonomous workflow agents."

## Trade-offs

Outcome-first copy can feel less differentiated to technical buyers — address with a secondary "how" section and architecture credibility signals.
